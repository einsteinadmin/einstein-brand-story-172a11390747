Einstein Moving Company

Brand Story Inspiration

10 companies that took unglamorous services and made their website the brand experience. What we can steal for Einstein's redesign.

Prepared by Albert · April 2026 · Scroll to explore ↓

The Starting Point

Einstein reads better than it looks.

The copy is best-in-class — witty, warm, confident. Zoolander refs and "tiny math elves." But the visual design is generic 2020-era WordPress. The site doesn't match the personality.

What's Strong

Brand voice. Trust signals (4.9/5.0, 5,900+ reviews, 55+ awards). The awards page. The new quote form.

What's Dated

Visual design could be any service company. 678ms TTFB. No personalization. Homepage is long but unfocused. Mobile doesn't delight.

The Gap

Newer pages (awards, quote form) feel like 2026. Core pages (home, about, services) feel like 2021. The gap grows with every new page built on the old foundation.

01 · Toilet Paper

Who Gives A Crap

whogivesacrap.org ~$100M+ Revenue · DTC · 50% profits donated

Why It's Great

They sell toilet paper and made it a lifestyle brand. The humor is everywhere — even their cart error says "Oh, crap!" Custom illustrations and bold colors transform the most boring product on earth into something you want to Instagram. They lead with mission (WHY), not product (WHAT).

Steal This

Personality in Every Pixel

Einstein's humor shouldn't be confined to the About page. It should be in the quote confirmation, the loading states, the 404 page, the footer. WGAC proves every micro-moment is a brand moment.

Steal This

Mission Integration

Einstein's 55+ awards and community impact should be woven into the experience — not listed on a trophy page nobody visits.

02 · Insurance

Lemonade

lemonade.com NYSE: LMND · Home, Renters, Pet, Car Insurance

Why It's Great

"Forget everything you know about insurance." That hot pink CTA on a hand-drawn illustration background — you know it's Lemonade before you read a word. They obliterated every insurance design convention. Custom illustrations replace stock photos. Maya (AI chatbot) makes quoting feel like texting, not paperwork.

Steal This

Own a Color

Lemonade owns pink in insurance. Einstein's orange should be just as instantly recognizable in moving. The current site doesn't commit hard enough to the palette.

Steal This

Conversational UX

"Tell us about your move" hits different than "Request a Quote." Small copy changes shift the whole vibe from form to conversation.

03 · Health Insurance

Oscar Health

hioscar.com NYSE: OSCR · 1M+ Members · 22 States

Why It's Great

"Health insurance made for real life." Six words that reframe the entire category. That serif on bold purple feels like a lifestyle brand, not a claims processor. Real people, real emotions in every photo — "fun but grown up, with a handmade feel." And their bravest brand attribute? "Imperfect."

Steal This

Serif Typography for Warmth

Most moving sites use generic sans-serif. A well-chosen serif paired with a clean sans could instantly signal "we're different" while feeling warmer and more editorial. Look at that headline font.

Steal This

"Partner" Not "Provider"

"We're your moving partner" hits different than "Request our services." Oscar's language of partnership builds relationship before the transaction even starts.

04 · Restaurants · 11 Locations

Dishoom

dishoom.com ~$375M Valuation · LVMH-backed · Bombay-Inspired Cafes

Why It's Great

A restaurant website that reads like a novel. Cinematic video hero with serif typography on a dark palette. Every single location has a unique fictional backstory set in 1960s Bombay. Even the cookie notice has personality: "The best cookies are for dipping. Preferably into a warming cup of our House Chai." The scroll unfolds narratively — no aggressive CTAs.

Steal This

Location Pages with Soul

Einstein has 10 branches across Texas and Florida. Each city has a vibe. The Austin page shouldn't look like the Tampa page. Local photography, local references, local personality. Dishoom does this with 11 locations.

Steal This

Scroll as Story, Not Conversion Pressure

What if the homepage led with the origin story — two kindergarten friends, one truck, a dream — and the quote came after you already loved them?

05 · Handyman Services

Honey Homes

honeyhomes.com 3,000+ Members · VC-backed · Multi-market

Why It's Great

"Your home, handled." Three words that reframe handyman work as peace of mind. Proof stacking is immediate: "less than a 1% pass rate," 4.9 stars, Yahoo/WSJ/TechCrunch logos — all above the fold. Photography shows clean, professional humans, not stock wrenches. The scroll follows a narrative arc: Problem → Solution → Benefits → Pricing → Proof → Guarantee.

Steal This

Proof Stacking Above the Fold

Einstein has BETTER proof than almost any moving company: 5,900+ reviews, 4.9 stars, 55+ awards, Inc. 5000 x7. Stack it aggressively. Don't make people scroll to find it. Make it impossible to miss.

Steal This

Vetting as a Story

"Less than 1% pass rate" is a stat that tells a story about quality. Einstein could tell the mover hiring and training story the same way — how selective they are, why their movers are different.

06 · Men's Grooming

Dr. Squatch

drsquatch.com Acquired by Unilever for $1.5B · Men's Soap & Grooming

Why It's Great

They turned soap into a cultural hype cycle. Dragon Ball Z limited editions. Scent families as personality expression. Their banned ingredients page is called the "sh*t list." The YouTube ad that launched the brand ("You're Not a Dish, You're a Man") has 100M+ views. Even out-of-stock messages say "Aww crap." Humor is everywhere without undermining credibility.

Steal This

Voice in Every Micro-Moment

Rewards programs, out-of-stock messages, ingredient lists — all have personality. Einstein's voice should show up in the quote confirmation, the "we're on our way" text, the post-move survey. Not just the homepage.

Steal This

Video as Brand-Builder

The Squatch origin video is legendary. Einstein has Austin L making content. A 60-second brand film — the kindergarten story, the energy, the humor — that's homepage-hero material.

07 · Bathroom Products

TUSHY

hellotushy.com ~$50M+ Revenue · DTC Bidets

Why It's Great

They sell bidets and they lean into it. Self-aware humor about selling butt-cleaning products. Bold color palette and playful design make a bidet look like a premium design object. Every piece of copy acknowledges the absurdity, which makes the brand feel confident and human. Education through entertainment.

Steal This

Lean Into the Unglamorous

Moving IS stressful, sweaty, chaotic. Instead of pretending it's Pinterest-perfect, own the real moments — the box that's too heavy, the couch that won't fit, the kid who won't stop "helping." Humor about reality builds more trust than polished stock photos.

Steal This

Confidence = Not Taking Yourself Too Seriously

TUSHY sells bidets and laughs about it. Einstein could acknowledge that moving sucks — and that's exactly why you want people who know how to make it not suck.

08 · Fast-Casual Food · 220+ Locations

Sweetgreen

sweetgreen.com NYSE: SG · ~$650M Revenue

Why It's Great

"SUNSHINE IN A SALAD" — aspirational headline that elevates lettuce into a lifestyle. Custom typeface (SweetSans) gives them a typographic identity no competitor can replicate. Ingredient transparency is woven into product descriptions, not hidden. Editorial design: generous whitespace, bold type, magazine-quality photography. The site is 70% air. It breathes.

Steal This

Custom Typography as Brand DNA

Every other moving company uses the same three fonts. Own one. A carefully selected or custom typeface would be a massive differentiator for Einstein.

Steal This

Whitespace and Restraint

Most moving sites cram every service, badge, and testimonial above the fold. Einstein could let the brand breathe. What you leave out matters as much as what you put in.

09 · B2B Video Platform

Wistia

wistia.com ~$100M+ Revenue · Self-Funded · ~250 Employees

Why It's Great

"One place for all your team's video-ing" — playful verb construction that no corporate brand would dare. Video hero (they eat their own cooking). Warm soft blues and pinks for a B2B company. Custom typeface. The key insight: they know when to be fun and when to get out of the way. Product pages are clean; culture pages go full personality.

Steal This

Fun Where It Counts, Clean Where It Converts

Homepage and About = pure personality. Quote form and booking flow = frictionless and clear. Don't sacrifice conversion for cleverness. The right balance is intentional.

Steal This

Bootstrap Pride as Brand Story

Wistia makes "self-funded" a badge of honor. Einstein's "two guys, one truck" energy is the same — that bootstrap origin is a brand asset. Make "started with one truck" equally prominent.

10 · Fast-Casual Food · 275+ Locations

Shake Shack

shakeshack.com NYSE: SHAK · ~$1B+ Revenue · Pentagram-designed

Why It's Great

Origin story IS the brand: a hot dog cart in Madison Square Park created to support a public art installation. That story gets told everywhere — not hidden. Pentagram designed the entire identity system: logo, signage, bags, uniforms, environmental graphics. 1950s neon-sign-inspired typography is unmistakable. "Stand For Something Good" is their values page. Danny Meyer's philosophy of "enlightened hospitality" (employees first, then guests, then community) permeates everything.

Steal This

Origin Story as Homepage Centerpiece

Shake Shack's "hot dog cart" story is on par with Einstein's "two kindergarten friends, one truck." Both are founder myths that deserve the spotlight. Don't bury it.

Steal This

Design System That Scales

10 branches = 10 opportunities. A unified but flexible design system — consistent brand, local personality — makes the website feel cohesive while each market page feels authentic. Shake Shack does this across 275 locations.

Steal This

"Fine-Dining Service in Work Boots"

Einstein's version of "enlightened hospitality": white-glove service from people in work boots. The contrast between the care level and the industry expectation IS the story. Lean into it.

Steal This

Every Surface is a Brand Canvas

Truck livery, mover uniforms, box branding, the website — they should all feel like one system. Shake Shack treats every surface (bags, cups, menus, walls) as brand expression.

The Playbook

7 Moves Einstein Should Make

Based on all 10 examples — what the best brand-story websites do that our current site doesn't.

01

Origin Story Front and Center

Two kindergarten best friends, one truck. First scroll, not page 3. Every great brand site leads with WHY, not WHAT.

02

Personality in Every Pixel

The copy voice is already there. The design needs to match it. Custom typography, committed color palette, photography that feels uniquely Einstein.

03

Proof Stacking, Not Proof Hiding

5,900+ reviews at 4.9 stars. 55+ awards. Inc. 5000 x7. That's absurd for a moving company. Stack it aggressively above the fold. Make it impossible to miss.

04

Video as Hero

Moving is physical, emotional, human. A homepage hero reel of real movers, real families, real moments — with that Einstein humor — would differentiate instantly.

05

Location Pages with Soul

10 markets = 10 opportunities to tell local stories. Local photography, local team features, local voice. Austin ≠ Tampa.

06

Voice in the Micro-Moments

Quote confirmation. 404 page. Loading screen. Post-move email. That's where brand magic happens — the moments nobody designs that everyone remembers.

07

Scroll as Narrative

Stop cramming everything above the fold. The best sites unfold like stories: problem → promise → proof → personality → convert. Let Einstein's story breathe.

The Bottom Line
"Einstein reads better than it looks. A redesign should close that gap."

The brand story is already incredible — two kindergarten best friends, one truck, 55+ awards, 5,900+ reviews at 4.9 stars. The website just needs to tell that story as well as the copy already does.
